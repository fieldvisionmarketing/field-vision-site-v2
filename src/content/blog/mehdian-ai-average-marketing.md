<strong>Key Takeaway:</strong> When you let AI write your positioning and strategy, it hands you the Mehdian: the statistical middle of everything anyone has ever said, which reads as average and forgettable. AI is built to produce the most probable answer, and the most probable answer is the one your competitors are already giving. Getting off the median takes human judgment, not another prompt.
</div>

Open every competitor's website in your category, one browser tab after another, and read the headlines back to back. "The platform that scales with you." "Built for modern teams." "Powering the future of whatever." You cannot tell them apart. Not because these companies actually do the same thing, but because they all asked the same three or four AI models to write their marketing, and the models all pointed at the same spot.

I have a name for that spot. The Mehdian.

## What Is a Mehdian?

A Mehdian is the average, middle-of-the-road result you get when AI writes your marketing. It is a portmanteau of two words: "meh" and "median." Median, because a language model is a machine for finding the middle. Meh, because the middle of everything is, by definition, unremarkable.

Feed your positioning through a model and it comes back technically fine, grammatically clean, and completely forgettable. Here is the important part: that is not the AI malfunctioning. That is the AI doing exactly what it was built to do.

## Why AI Regresses to the Middle

A large language model does one thing, over and over. It predicts the most probable next word.

"Most probable" sounds like a synonym for "best." It is not. The most probable phrasing is the most common phrasing. The most common phrasing is the one that already exists ten thousand times over in the training data, which is to say, the exact phrasing your competitors already used. Ask a model to describe your product and it will drift, sentence by sentence, toward the center of everything that has ever been written about products like yours.

In most parts of life, landing in the middle is safe. In marketing, the middle is a death sentence. Average is not a small tax you pay for convenience. Average is invisible. If your message sits in the dead center of your category, buyers have no way to tell you apart from the four other startups on their shortlist, and a prospect who cannot tell you apart will default to the cheapest option or the one they already know. Being forgettable is not a minor cosmetic problem. It is the whole problem.

## Where the Mehdian Does the Most Damage

AI is genuinely useful for plenty of marketing work. It can spin up a hundred subject lines, tighten a messy brief, summarize a call in seconds. Use it for that. Please.

The Mehdian does its real damage in the two places that are supposed to make you different: your positioning and your strategy. Those are not volume problems you solve by generating more. They are judgment problems you solve by choosing.

Positioning is the deliberate decision to stand somewhere specific and give up everywhere else. It only works because of what it excludes. A model cannot exclude anything. It is trained to cover the whole distribution, to hedge toward the safe center, to sand off the one sharp edge that would have made you memorable. So it writes positioning that offends no one and moves no one. It writes a strategy that reads like a summary of every strategy, because that is literally what it is. This is the same reason [AI slop feels so lifeless once you learn what taste actually means](https://fieldvisiongroup.com/blog/ai-slop-what-taste-means/): the machine optimizes for the average, and taste is the refusal to accept it.

## The Tell

You already know if this happened to you, because the tell is a quiet one.

Your messaging reads fine when you proofread it and does absolutely nothing when it ships. Nobody argues with it. Nobody repeats it. Nobody quotes it back to you. Three weeks after launch you cannot find a single customer who can describe what you do in their own words. That is the Mehdian. It does not fail loudly with a typo or a broken link. It fails silently, by refusing to land.

## The Way Off the Median

The way off the median is not a better prompt. It is a human who is willing to make a call the machine never will.

The moves that make a brand distinct are the low-probability ones. The sharp claim. The narrow audience you choose on purpose. The thing everyone in your category is too cautious to say out loud. AI is built to avoid exactly those, because they are improbable by definition, and improbable is the one thing a probability engine is designed to skip. Somebody has to decide that the brand is going to plant a flag on one specific hill and defend it while everyone else spreads themselves thin. That decision is what a seasoned marketing leader is for.

It is also why the fix for bland AI output is almost never more output. When founders feel their marketing is not working, the instinct is to make more of it. Usually the real issue is not a shortage of hands or content. It is a shortage of senior judgment about what to say and what to give up. That is the difference between [a marketing problem and a marketing leadership problem](https://fieldvisiongroup.com/blog/fractional-cmo-marketing-leadership-gap/), and the Mehdian is what a leadership gap looks like once AI fills the vacuum.

At Field Vision we call the goal Growth Without Guesswork, and here is the irony. AI gets sold as the end of guesswork, but for positioning and strategy it quietly smuggles guesswork back in. It guesses the average and hands it to you with total confidence and perfect grammar. The job of a real marketing leader is to replace that confident average with a deliberate choice.
