Searches for "fractional CMO vs. full-time CMO" were up 900% year over year in Google Ads Keyword Planner data I pulled in October. So if you're asking the question, you're not alone.

Most comparisons I read focus on the wrong variables. The answer I trust depends on one thing: what job marketing leadership needs to do for your company over the next 12 months.

I've been on both sides of this. I was a full-time marketing leader for over a decade: Pandora's first marketer, then building integrated marketing at Twitch, launching Hard Rock Bet, and starting Audience Development at Amazon as a team of one and growing it into a 30+ person global org. Now I'm a fractional CMO for music, sports, and gaming startups. Here's how I'd make the call.

## What Is a Fractional CMO?

A fractional CMO is a senior marketing executive who embeds with your team part-time, usually two to three days a week, and owns marketing strategy and the system that executes it. The good ones are hands-on. They set direction, build the playbooks and dashboards, hire and coach the team, and stay accountable for results.

## What Is a Full-Time CMO?

A full-time CMO is a dedicated executive who leads marketing every day as part of the company's leadership team. They run the marketing organization, manage a larger team, and own the function long-term.

## The Real Question: What Job Does Marketing Leadership Need to Do?

Marketing leadership at a startup does one of three jobs, and they come in order.

**Job 1: Set direction.** Who exactly are we for? Why do we win? What's the launch plan? Which moments, channels, and audiences do we bet on? This is judgment work, and it's where most early-stage marketing goes sideways.

**Job 2: Build the engine.** Turn that direction into a system the team can run: playbooks, dashboards, an operating cadence, lifecycle programs, a hiring plan. This is where a company stops depending on heroics.

**Job 3: Run the engine at scale.** Lead a growing team inside a system that already works, every single day, across more channels, markets, and products.

A fractional CMO is built for Jobs 1 and 2. A full-time CMO is built for Job 3.

Problems start when companies hire for the wrong job. Bring in a full-time executive before the direction is set, and you're asking someone to scale a system that doesn't exist yet. Bring in a fractional CMO when you need a leader running a 40-person team every day, and you'll feel the gaps.

## Fractional CMO vs. Full-Time CMO: Side by Side

| | Fractional CMO | Full-Time CMO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Setting direction and building the system (Jobs 1 and 2) | Running a working system at scale (Job 3) |
| Time to senior judgment | Weeks: embedded quickly, usually 2 to 3 days a week | Months: a full executive search and ramp |
| Range of pattern recognition | Wide: has seen many companies, categories, and stages | Deep: focused on one company over years |
| What the team keeps | Playbooks, dashboards, hiring plans, and a cadence the team owns | A leader who carries the system with them |
| Typical stage | Pre-launch through early scale, or a reset after growth stalls | Repeatable growth with a growing team |
| How it ends | Planned handoff to the team or a full-time hire | Ongoing |

The row I care about most is "What the team keeps." A fractional engagement should leave the company stronger than it found it, even after the CMO walks out the door.

## When a Fractional CMO Is the Right First Move

Founders usually come to a fractional CMO at one of these moments:

- **You just raised and need a plan.** The board expects growth, and nobody senior owns how you'll get it.
- **A launch has to land.** A new product, a new market, a new state. One shot, fixed date.
- **Growth stalled and nobody knows why.** Plenty of activity, no clear diagnosis.
- **The founder is still the CMO.** Every campaign and positioning call runs through the CEO.

In all four cases, you need judgment now and a system soon. Waiting months for the perfect full-time hire means making those calls without a senior leader, or making them twice.

## When You Need a Full-Time CMO

Fractional isn't the right answer forever.

You need a full-time CMO when marketing is a daily, high-volume operation: a large team, many channels running at once, multiple markets or product lines, and a leadership team that needs a marketing executive in every meeting. At that point the job is running the engine, and that takes someone in the seat full-time.

## Where Fractional CMOs Fail, and How to Avoid It

The fair criticism of fractional CMOs is that some of them are just advisors. They show up, give opinions, and leave nothing behind. The company still has no one who owns marketing.

A fractional engagement fails when:

- **The CMO doesn't own outcomes.** If they aren't accountable for a real number, they're a consultant.
- **There's no team to build or hand off to.** Strategy with nobody to run it becomes a slide deck.
- **Nothing is written down.** If the playbook lives in one person's head, it leaves with them.

## The Handoff: Fractional First, Then Full-Time

The best outcome I know is a fractional CMO who makes the full-time hire easier.

That looks like this: the fractional CMO sets direction, builds the engine, proves what works, defines the full-time role based on what the company now actually needs, helps hire that person, and hands over a working system on day one. The new CMO walks into a team with a plan and a scoreboard.

## Takeaways

- **Decide based on the job,** whether that's setting direction, building the engine, or running it at scale.
- **Fractional fits Jobs 1 and 2.** Full-time fits Job 3.
- **Watch the "what the team keeps" row.** A fractional engagement should leave playbooks, dashboards, and a hiring plan behind.
- **Avoid advisor-only fractional CMOs.** Demand ownership of outcomes and a planned handoff.
- **The strongest path is often fractional first, then full-time,** with the fractional CMO helping hire their successor.
