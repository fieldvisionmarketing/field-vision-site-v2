Sports brands attract a specific kind of marketing mistake. Because the category is exciting, founders assume marketing is about big creative swings: the launch film, the athlete partnership, the stunt at the game. So they hire for creative horsepower or sign an agency that can produce a splashy campaign. Then six months later the brand has run some memorable content and still cannot tell you which fan segment it is building or why.

The problem is not creativity. It is that sports marketing is fandom marketing, and fandom is an identity relationship, not a purchase funnel. Fans do not "convert" once. They attach, return, defend the brand to other people, and spend across tickets, merch, media, and betting over years. A marketing leader who has only run performance campaigns for standard consumer products will optimize the wrong thing. They will chase installs or signups and miss the actual asset, which is a fan base that compounds.

This is what to look for in a marketing leader for a sports brand, and how to decide whether you need a fractional CMO, an agency, or a full-time hire.

## The judgment a sports brand actually needs

Before you evaluate any candidate, get clear on the specific fluencies that separate a sports marketing leader from a strong generalist. There are four.

**Fan acquisition and lifecycle.** The goal is not a one-time sale. It is a fan who comes back, brings others, and spends in multiple ways over time. A real sports marketing leader thinks in audience development and lifecycle, building systems that turn a first interaction into lasting fandom. This is the difference between renting attention with ad spend and building an audience the brand owns.

**Partnership, sponsorship, and league dynamics.** Sports is a relationship business. Growth often runs through teams, leagues, venues, athletes, and media partners whose incentives do not automatically align with yours. A leader who has built and run those partnerships knows how to structure a deal and a campaign that works for everyone at the table, and how to turn a partnership into fan acquisition instead of just a logo on a banner.

**Live, venue, and broadcast moments.** The most powerful fan-conversion events in sports happen around live games, venues, and broadcasts. A leader who has activated at that level knows how to use a game, a season, or a broadcast window as a growth engine, not just a marketing beat on the calendar.

**Regulated and emerging adjacencies.** Many sports brands touch categories with their own rules and dynamics: sports betting, fantasy, NIL, ticketing, and collectibles. A leader who has operated inside a regulated category like betting understands the compliance, the acquisition economics, and the referral mechanics that make or break growth there.

## What to look for when you evaluate candidates

When you screen a marketing leader for a sports brand, look for evidence in five areas rather than a familiar list of channels.

They have built fan audiences inside sports or the broader fan economy, not just optimized ad accounts. Ask for a specific fan base they grew and exactly how. They can name the strategic decision, not just the tactic. You want someone who says "we chose this fan segment and this moment because," not "we ran these campaigns." They understand partnerships as an acquisition engine, not a branding exercise. They build systems that outlast them, so the brand keeps compounding after they step back. And they have senior pattern recognition, because they have seen enough sports and entertainment growth up close to tell durable strategy from a stunt that spikes and fades.

If you already have people producing marketing work but no one setting direction, you do not have an execution shortage. You have a [marketing leadership gap](/blog/fractional-cmo-marketing-leadership-gap/), and only senior judgment closes it.

## Fractional CMO, agency, or full-time: which model fits

The model matters as much as the person. Here is how the three common options compare for a sports brand.

| Model | What it gives you | Where it falls short for sports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-time CMO | Dedicated, always-on senior leadership | Costly and slow to hire before the fan-growth engine is proven, and a wrong hire burns a year |
| Marketing agency | Execution across specific channels and creative | Runs the channels you assign, but will not tell you which fan segment or moment to build around |
| Fractional CMO | Senior strategic judgment on a focused schedule, applied to your specifics | Not a fit once marketing is a daily, high-volume operation needing full-time ownership |

For most sports and sports-tech brands from early revenue through Series B, the right first move is a senior leader or [fractional CMO](/services/fractional-cmo/) who sets strategy, paired with an agency or in-house team who executes it. The fractional model exists to install a strategy and a system and then hand it off, following the [ABCT sequence](/guides/abct-framework/): Audit, Blueprint, Construct, Transfer. You graduate to a full-time CMO when marketing has become a daily operation that needs an executive in the seat every day.

## Proof this works in sports specifically

This is the terrain Field Vision founder David Hampian has operated in directly. At Twitch, he created the co-streaming model for NFL games, a creator-led approach to sports broadcasting later echoed across the industry, and he scaled Music and Sports from 2 percent to 8.1 percent of all new acquisitions. He led Twitch's founding partnership with the Las Vegas Raiders and opened the Twitch Lounge at Allegiant Stadium, Twitch's first physical brand space. At Hard Rock, he took a legacy brick-and-mortar brand with no digital presence and launched Florida's first legal online sportsbook, building the brand from scratch, launching across six states, and exceeding the acquisition forecast by 232 percent at a 2x return on ad spend. That is the difference between someone who has marketed near sports and someone who has built fan acquisition inside it.

## What to do next

If you are hiring marketing leadership for a sports brand, do three things. Diagnose whether your real gap is execution or judgment before you hire anyone. Choose the model that fits your stage, which for most sports brands means senior strategy first, execution second. And screen for a leader who has built fan audiences and run partnerships inside the sports ecosystem, not just managed ad accounts.

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Buying execution before strategy is how sports brands waste a year producing activity that never builds a fan base.

<h3 itemprop="name">What makes sports marketing different from general consumer marketing?</h3>
<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">

Sports marketing is built on fandom and identity, which behaves differently from standard consumer purchasing. Growth runs through fan communities, partnerships with teams, leagues, and venues, live and broadcast moments, and often regulated adjacent categories like betting. A leader who has operated inside the sports ecosystem understands these levers; a generalist treats them as ordinary channels.

<div itemscope itemprop="acceptedAnswer" itemtype="https://schema.org/Answer">
<div itemprop="text">
