Music founders ask me some version of the same question every few weeks: "How do I know if a marketing leader actually understands music?" It's a fair question. Most fractional CMOs come from B2B SaaS or e-commerce, and a music app breaks almost every assumption those playbooks are built on.

I've spent most of my career inside music products. I was the first growth marketing hire at Pandora, where we built the lifecycle program that helped grow it to over 80 million monthly active users. I built Industry Marketing connecting Pandora to Ticketfly, worked on Amazon Music Live and Maestro at Amazon, and led Twitch's expansion into music. Here's what I'd look for if I were hiring for your seat.

## Why Is a Music App Different to Market?

Three things set music apps apart from almost every other consumer product:

| Music app reality | What it means for marketing |
|---|---|
| Listening is a daily habit | Retention and lifecycle matter more than one-time conversion |
| Catalog, rights and artists shape the product | Positioning and partnerships run through the music industry |
| Fandom drives discovery | Artist and fan loops often beat paid acquisition |

A marketer who misses any of these will optimize the wrong number. The most common version: a big download chart and a day-7 retention curve nobody wants to talk about.

## Five Things to Look For

### 1. They've grown a music or audio product

Ask what music, audio or streaming products they've personally grown, and what their role was. Running a campaign for a label is useful experience. Owning the growth of a listening product is a different job, and it's the one you're hiring for.

### 2. They talk about retention first

Ask how they'd measure success in the first 90 days. A strong answer centers on first-week activation and week-two retention. At Pandora, we found two early behaviors that predicted long-term listeners, and building the first week around them lifted week-two retention 21%. Someone who has done this work will ask about your activation behaviors in the first conversation.

### 3. They understand artists as a growth channel

Artists bring audiences. The best music marketers treat artist and creator programs as acquisition infrastructure. At Pandora and Ticketfly, connecting listening data to live events drove 16 million ticket sales and promoted over 90,000 concerts, because fans showed up where their artists were.

### 4. They can tie cultural moments to growth

Music runs on moments: releases, tours, award shows, festivals. Ask how they'd turn a moment into lasting users. At Amazon Music Live, turning one-off events into a repeatable audience engine drove 31% customer growth and 39% revenue growth.

### 5. They build systems your team keeps

A fractional CMO should leave you with a working machine: dashboards, a testing cadence, a lifecycle program and a team that knows how to run it. Ask what they hand over at the end of an engagement. If the answer is a strategy deck, keep looking.

## Questions to Ask in the Interview

- What music or audio products have you grown, and what did you own?
- What were the retention numbers before and after your work?
- How would you find our activation behaviors?
- How have you worked with artists, labels or creators to drive growth?
- What does our team own when you leave?

## Red Flags

- **Downloads as the headline metric.** Installs without retention are a cost.
- **A paid-first plan.** Paid media scales a working funnel. It rarely fixes a broken one.
- **No point of view on catalog or rights.** Licensing shapes what you can say and where you can launch.
- **Generic case studies.** A SaaS pipeline story tells you little about listener habits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I look for in a fractional CMO for a music app?

Look for someone who has grown a music or audio product before, can show retention results alongside acquisition results, understands artists, catalog and rights, and can build a system your team keeps running after the engagement ends.

### What does a fractional CMO do for a music app?

A fractional CMO for a music app owns the marketing strategy part time, usually 2 to 3 days a week. That covers positioning, listener acquisition, first-week activation and retention, free-to-paid or subscription monetization, artist and partner programs, and building the team that executes.

### Why does music app marketing need a specialist?

Music apps are habit products shaped by catalog, licensing and artists. Retention matters more than one-time conversion, and fandom often outperforms paid media. A marketer who has only worked in B2B SaaS or e-commerce tends to optimize the wrong numbers.
