<strong>Key Takeaway:</strong> To hire a marketing leader for a music brand, hire for judgment before hands. The hard problems in music (which fan segments to build, how to work streaming platforms and label relationships, whether to bet on catalog or new releases) are strategy calls, not execution tasks. Start with a senior leader or fractional CMO who has built audiences inside the music ecosystem, let them set the strategy and the shortlist of channels, then add the agency or junior hires to run it.
</div>

If you run a music brand, a music-tech startup, or a label services company, the instinct when growth stalls is to hire someone to "do marketing." A social manager. An agency. A growth generalist off a startup job board. That instinct is usually a step out of order. The first hire that changes the trajectory of a music brand is not more hands on the channels. It is the person who decides which channels matter, in what order, and why.

Music is one of the hardest categories to market well, because it does not behave like standard consumer marketing. You are not just moving someone from awareness to purchase. You are building fandom, and fandom compounds through streaming platforms, artist and label relationships, live moments, and catalog that keeps earning for years. A marketing leader who has only run performance campaigns for SaaS or ecommerce will treat a music brand like a funnel. It is not a funnel. It is an audience you develop over time.

This is a guide to hiring the person who gets that difference, and to choosing the right model (fractional CMO, agency, or full-time hire) for the stage you are actually at.

## The three ways to lead marketing for a music brand

There are three common models for putting marketing leadership into a music company, and founders often reach for the wrong one because it is the fastest to buy, not because it fits the problem.

| Model | What it gives you | Where it falls short for music |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-time CMO | Dedicated, always-on senior leadership | Expensive and slow to hire before the growth engine is proven, and a bad hire costs a year of runway |
| Marketing agency | Execution capacity across specific channels | Optimizes the channels you assign, but will not tell you that you picked the wrong ones for a music audience |
| Fractional CMO | Senior strategic judgment on a focused schedule, applied to your specifics | Not a fit once marketing is a daily, high-volume operation that needs full-time ownership |

The pattern most music brands miss is that these are not either-or. The highest-leverage setup at the early stage is usually a senior leader who sets strategy plus an agency or in-house team who executes it. The [fractional CMO](/services/fractional-cmo/) is the brain that aims the hands. Buying hands first, with no one to aim them, is how music companies spend twelve months producing activity that never adds up to an audience.

## What a music brand actually needs from a marketing leader

Generic marketing leadership is not enough here. The person leading marketing for a music brand needs specific fluency in how music audiences actually grow. Four areas separate someone who can lead a music brand from someone who has simply run campaigns.

**Audience development, not just acquisition.** In music, the goal is not a one-time conversion. It is a fan who listens, returns, shares, and eventually pays in multiple ways: streaming, merch, tickets, superfan products. A real music marketing leader thinks in lifecycle and audience development, building systems that turn a first listen into a lasting relationship. This is the difference between renting attention with ad spend and building an audience you own.

**Streaming platform and DSP dynamics.** Growth in music is shaped by how you show up on Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, and the rest, plus editorial, algorithmic, and playlist behavior that shifts constantly. A leader who understands platform mechanics can build a strategy that works with the platforms instead of fighting them. A generalist will treat these as "channels" and miss the game entirely.

**Artist, label, and rights relationships.** Music is a relationship business. Marketing a music brand often means coordinating with artists, managers, labels, and rights holders whose incentives do not automatically line up with yours. The right leader has operated inside those relationships and knows how to build campaigns that work for everyone at the table.

**Live and catalog thinking.** Live moments and ticketing are among the most powerful fan-conversion events in music, and catalog is an asset that earns for years. A leader who has worked across live, ticketing, and catalog knows how to use a tour, a release, or a rediscovered back catalog as a growth engine, not just a marketing beat.

## What to look for when you hire

When you evaluate a marketing leader for a music brand, screen for evidence in five areas rather than a list of channels they have run.

They have built audiences inside the music or fan economy, not just optimized ad accounts. Ask for a specific audience they grew and how they did it. They can name the strategic call, not just the tactic. The signal you want is someone who says "we chose this segment and this platform because," not "we ran these campaigns." They think in systems that outlast them. The best leaders build the strategy, the measurement, and the team so the company keeps compounding after they step back. They can direct an agency and a team, not replace them. You want judgment layered on top of the execution you already have. And they have senior pattern recognition, because they have seen enough music and entertainment growth up close to know what works and what only looks like it works.

The [difference between a marketing problem and a marketing leadership gap](/blog/fractional-cmo-marketing-leadership-gap/) is the single most useful lens here. If you already have people producing marketing work but no one setting direction, you do not have a hands problem. You have a judgment problem, and only senior leadership fixes it.

## When a fractional CMO is the right call for music

For most music brands and music-tech startups between early revenue and Series B, a fractional CMO is the right first form of marketing leadership. You get chief-marketing-officer-level judgment applied to your specifics, without committing to a full-time executive before the growth engine is proven. The engagement is built to install a strategy and a system, then transfer it to your team. That is the whole point of the [ABCT model](/guides/abct-framework/): Audit what you have, Blueprint the strategy, Construct the systems and team, then Transfer ownership so you are not dependent on any one person.

You move to a full-time CMO when marketing has become a daily, high-volume operation that needs an executive in the seat every day. Until then, the fractional model gives you the seniority a music brand needs at the exact moment it needs direction most.

## Proof this works in music specifically

This is the world Field Vision founder David Hampian has operated in for his entire career. At Pandora, he was the first hire on the growth team and helped scale the platform from 50 million to more than 80 million monthly active users, building lifecycle marketing from zero. When Pandora acquired Rdio and TicketFly to enter live music, he built the industry marketing function and helped launch and scale artist, label, and ticketing marketing that sold 16 million tickets. At Amazon, he led the marketing launch of Maestro, Amazon Music's first generative-AI product, growing monthly active users by 124 percent and listening hours by 41 percent through lifecycle tactics. That is the difference between someone who has marketed music and someone who has built the audiences music brands depend on.

## What to do next

If you are deciding how to lead marketing for a music brand, do three things. Diagnose whether you have a marketing problem or a leadership gap before you hire anyone. Choose the model that fits your stage, which for most early music brands means a senior leader or fractional CMO setting strategy with an agency or team executing. And screen candidates for evidence of building audiences inside the music ecosystem, not just running campaigns.
