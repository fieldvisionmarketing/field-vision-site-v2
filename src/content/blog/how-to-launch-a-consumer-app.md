<strong>Key Takeaway:</strong> A consumer app launch is decided in the first week of each user's life, long before the install count matters. Pick one audience, seed the content they came for, find the two or three early behaviors that predict a returning user, and build the whole launch around them. Scale acquisition only after retention holds.

Most consumer app launches I see follow the same script. The product is good. The founders raise a round. They line up a launch week, buy a burst of installs, post the download chart on LinkedIn, and then watch the day-7 curve fall off a cliff.

I've been on the other side of this problem my whole career. I was the first growth marketing hire at Pandora, where we built the lifecycle program that helped grow it to over 80 million monthly active users. At Twitch, I led the expansion into music and sports. At Amazon, I ran audience development across Amazon Music, Audible, Twitch and Prime Video. Every one of those is a consumer app, and every one of them lived or died on the same thing: did people come back?

Here's the 90-day playbook I use with founders launching music, streaming, creator, micro-drama and fan apps.

## What Makes a Consumer App Different?

A consumer app is a habit product. People open it because of what's inside it, and they keep it because opening it becomes part of their day. That changes the launch math in three ways:

| What changes | Why it matters at launch |
|---|---|
| Value compounds with use | A user who leaves in week one never sees what makes the app good |
| Supply drives demand | Creators, artists or shows recruit users better than ads do |
| Retention sets the ceiling | Every install you buy before retention works is money spent twice |

A SaaS launch can survive a leaky first month. A consumer app usually can't.

## Days 1 to 30: Before Launch

### Pick a beachhead audience

Write one sentence that names who the app is for. "Music fans" is a category. "College a cappella groups who want to share sets" is an audience. The narrower the first audience, the easier it is to find them, speak to them and get them talking to each other.

### Seed supply before demand

If your app depends on creators, artists, shows or communities, recruit them first. Empty rooms kill consumer apps. At Twitch, every new vertical started with the creators who would anchor it, because viewers follow the people they already watch. Aim to launch with enough content that a new user's first session feels full.

### Choose a launch moment

Give your audience a reason to show up on a specific date. Tie the launch to a moment they already care about: a tour, a tournament, a season premiere, a cultural event. When we launched creator-led Thursday Night Football co-streams on Twitch, the game was the moment and the creators were the reason to watch there. Year-over-year viewership grew 73%.

## Days 31 to 60: Launch and the First Week

### Find your activation behaviors

Every consumer app has two or three early actions that predict who stays. Find them in your beta data before you scale. At Pandora, the two that mattered were creating more than one station and giving more than five thumbs in the first week. Users who did both kept listening for years.

### Design the first seven days around them

Once you know the behaviors, build onboarding, push, email and in-app prompts that guide every new user toward them. At Pandora, rebuilding the first-week journey around those two behaviors lifted week-two retention 21%.

### Read cohorts daily

During launch, the dashboard that matters is retention by acquisition source. You'll quickly see which channels bring users who stay and which bring tourists. Cut the tourist channels early.

## Days 61 to 90: Scale What Works

### Turn users into distribution

Build sharing into the product so the content travels: clips, playlists, recaps, invites, watch parties. Twitch Sells Out, a 36-hour creator-led livestream, drove $24M in revenue and 89K Prime sign-ups, and it was built entirely around creators and the communities that already followed them.

### Add paid acquisition last

Once day-7 retention holds for a channel, scale it. Paid media works well for a consumer app that keeps its users. It burns cash for one that doesn't.

### Sequence monetization after the habit

Subscriptions, coins, ads and commerce all work better once the habit is formed. At Pandora, swapping generic upgrade messages for behaviorally triggered upsells lifted subscription conversion 12%. Timing did the work.

## The 90-Day Launch Checklist

- **Write the beachhead sentence.** One audience, one reason to care.
- **Seed supply.** Enough content and creators that the first session feels full.
- **Pick the moment.** A date your audience already circles.
- **Name your activation behaviors.** Two or three, validated in beta data.
- **Build the first week.** Every message points toward those behaviors.
- **Read cohorts by source.** Daily during launch, weekly after.
- **Scale only what retains.** Then add paid, then monetization.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I launch a consumer app?

Pick one beachhead audience and one launch moment, seed the content and creators before launch, design the first week around the two or three behaviors that predict a returning user, and only then scale acquisition. Paid installs come last, after you know which users stay.

### How long does it take to launch a consumer app?

Plan on about 90 days: roughly 30 days of positioning and supply seeding before launch, a launch moment, and 60 days of first-week optimization and cohort reading afterward. Founders who skip the pre-launch month usually spend the next quarter buying installs that churn.

### What metrics matter most when launching a consumer app?

Day-1, day-7 and week-2 retention by acquisition source, the share of new users who complete your key activation behaviors in their first week, and content or creator supply per active user. Downloads are a vanity metric until those numbers hold.

### When should a consumer app hire a fractional CMO?

About 4 to 8 weeks before launch, or as soon as installs grow faster than retention. That gives a senior leader time to set positioning, pick the beachhead audience, seed supply and build first-week activation before money goes into paid acquisition.
