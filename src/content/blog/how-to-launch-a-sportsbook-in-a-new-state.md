<strong>Key Takeaway:</strong> A sportsbook state launch is won in two windows. The first is the stretch around go-live, when attention and sign-up intent peak and every competitor is spending at once. The second is a customer's first 90 days, when they either build a habit with you or leave with the bonus. Plan both before launch day. Tomorrow, Nebraska votes on [Initiative 440](https://ballotpedia.org/Nebraska_Online_Sports_Betting_Amendment_(2026)), a constitutional amendment that would let the legislature authorize online sports betting for people located in the state. If it passes, someone is going to run a state launch. Probably several someones.

I've run that play six times.

At Hard Rock Bet, we launched Florida's first legal online sportsbook, then took the same brand into Arizona, New Jersey, and beyond. We started with a legacy hospitality brand and no digital sportsbook brand at all. Our competition was DraftKings and FanDuel, who had already spent billions teaching America their names.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about a state launch: the launch itself is the easy part to plan. Everyone knows there's a go-live date. The part that decides whether you're still standing a year later is everything that happens before and after it.

So here's the playbook, start to finish.

## What Most Sportsbooks Get Wrong About a State Launch

Most launch plans I've seen make the same four mistakes.

**They treat launch as one big media moment.** A burst of spend on go-live day, then a cliff. Attention in a new state is concentrated into a short window, and you need a plan for the weeks on both sides of it.

**They buy switchers.** Sign-up bonuses are table stakes, and in this category they drive constant switching. If the bonus is your whole acquisition strategy, you're renting customers who will leave for the next bonus.

**They copy and paste the national campaign.** A state is a culture. Teams, rivalries, local media, local slang. A campaign that ignores that gets ignored back.

**They measure sign-ups.** Sign-ups are the easiest number to move. Report the value of the customers you keep, measured against what it took to win them.

## Phase 1: The 30 Days Before Go-Live

The month before launch is where you buy yourself an unfair launch week.

### Decide why anyone should pick you

You can't outspend the incumbents, so don't fight on their terms. Pick the one reason a bettor in this state should choose you and make every asset say it. For Hard Rock, that meant building a brand identity, creative system, and media strategy from zero around a name people already had feelings about.

### Build the local layer

Map the state's sports calendar, its teams, its rivalries, and the local voices people trust. Line up the partnerships and cultural moments you'll launch around. Localize the messaging before launch day, because there's no time to do it after.

### Clear compliance before you need it

Every offer, every piece of creative, every geolocation rule and responsible gaming message should be approved before go-live. A launch week spent waiting on approvals is a launch week your competitors get for free.

### Wire up measurement

Connect brand and performance spend to customer lifetime value before the first dollar goes out. If you can only see sign-ups on day one, you'll optimize for sign-ups on day two.

### Pick your activation behaviors now

Decide which early actions predict a valuable customer, and design launch week around driving them. More on this in Phase 3.

## Phase 2: Launch Week

Launch week is about concentration.

At Hard Rock Bet, each state got a coordinated launch: a brand awareness blitz, strategic media buys, and cultural tentpole activations, all stacked into a compressed window to capture as much attention as possible while it was available.

Three rules for the week:

1. **Stack everything into the window.** Paid, owned, partners, PR, and local activations should land together. Spread across a month, the same spend disappears.
2. **Anchor to a sports moment.** A season opener, a rivalry game, a big weekend. People are already paying attention to sports. Launch into that attention.
3. **Optimize in real time.** You'll learn more about the market in the first 72 hours than in the previous 30 days. Have the measurement and the authority in place to move spend daily.

## Phase 3: Days 1 to 90, Where the Business Is Decided

This is where most of the money gets made or lost, and it's where most launch plans go quiet.

In a category where sign-up bonuses drive switching, engagement beyond the first bet is the battleground. At Hard Rock Bet, we identified the activation behaviors that signaled a valuable customer and built campaigns to drive each one:

- **A second bet within 48 hours** of the first
- **Betting on more than one sport**
- **Live in-play wagering**

I learned this lesson long before sportsbooks. At Pandora, we found that two early listener actions, creating more than one station and giving more than five thumbs in the first week, predicted whether someone would stick around for years. We built the entire lifecycle program around getting new listeners to do those two things.

Every product has its version of those behaviors. Your job in the first 90 days is to find yours and build every email, push notification, in-app message, and offer around them.

Then measure what matters. At Hard Rock Bet, ARPU came in 17% above target. That came from customers who formed a habit in their first weeks.

## Phase 4: Make It Repeatable

The second state should be faster than the first. The sixth should feel routine.

We built a repeatable launch framework at Hard Rock Bet that we could localize for each market. Same structure, same phases, same measurement. What changed state to state was the messaging, the media mix, and the cultural hooks. That's what made a six-state rollout possible without rebuilding the plan every time.

Write your playbook down after launch one. Your team should own it, and you shouldn't need a hero to run launch two.

## What's Different for a State Launch in 2026

The biggest change since I ran these launches: you're no longer competing only with other sportsbooks.

During the first half of the World Cup in June 2026, Kalshi and Polymarket took **73.5% of new installs** among the major sportsbook and prediction market apps tracked by Apptopia ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/kalshi-polymarket-three-quarters-betting-app-downloads-during-world-cup-surge/)). DraftKings and FanDuel now run their own standalone prediction apps too, which Apptopia tracked separately.

That changes the launch math in two ways. First, many of your future customers already have a sports-trading app on their phone before you go live, which makes your reason to switch matter more than ever. Second, retention matters even more, because every one of those apps is one tap away. The 90-day plan stops being a nice-to-have.

## The State Launch Checklist

| Phase | What to lock | The number to watch |

| 30 days before | Positioning, local partners and moments, compliance approvals, LTV measurement, activation behaviors | Pre-launch interest in the state |
| Launch week | Stacked brand, media, partner and local activations anchored to a sports moment | Cost per valuable customer, daily |
| Days 1 to 90 | Lifecycle built around your activation behaviors | Second bet within 48 hours, multi-sport rate, ARPU |
| After launch one | A written playbook your team can localize and run | Time and effort to launch state two |

## Takeaways

- **A state launch has two windows:** go-live, and each customer's first 90 days. Plan both before launch day.
- **Don't fight the incumbents on their terms.** Give bettors in this state one clear reason to choose you.
- **Concentrate launch week** around a real sports moment, then optimize daily.
- **Build the first 90 days around activation behaviors** like a second bet within 48 hours.
- **Write it down** so the next state is faster than the last.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you launch a sportsbook in a new state?

Plan two windows. In the 30 days before go-live, lock positioning, local partners, compliance approvals, and lifetime-value measurement. During launch week, concentrate brand, media, and local activations around a sports moment. In each customer's first 90 days, drive the behaviors that predict value, such as a second bet within 48 hours. Then document the playbook so the next state is faster.

### What is the most important part of a sportsbook launch?

The first 90 days after a customer signs up. Sign-up bonuses drive switching in sports betting, so retention decides profitability. According to David Hampian, who launched Hard Rock Bet across six states, building engagement beyond the first bet, through behaviors like a second bet within 48 hours, multi-sport betting, and live in-play wagering, is where the business is won.

### How can a new sportsbook compete with DraftKings and FanDuel?

Avoid competing on their terms. A new entrant can't outspend incumbents that have spent billions on brand and acquisition. Pick one clear reason bettors in that state should choose you, localize everything to the state's teams and culture, concentrate launch spend into a short window, and win on retention.
