That former Hollywood star you assumed was on an island somewhere? They have a podcast.

The rapper who stopped by the Billboard charts for a cup of coffee in 2014? Podcast. Still mumbling.

Your neighbor from college who had a bong permanently attached to his face? Somehow, some way, a podcast.

Everyone has a podcast. Even me.

So when Netflix and Spotify announced a deal to put video podcasts on Netflix, nobody should have been surprised. What surprised me was how few brand leaders I talked to had thought through what it means for them.

Here is what it means: long-form content is back. Honestly, it never left. It just got talked out of the room by a lot of marketing meetings about Reels.

## What Netflix and Spotify Just Told Us About Video Podcasts

In October 2025, Spotify and Netflix announced a [video podcast partnership](https://awfulannouncing.com/ringer/netflix-spotify-video-podcasts-bill-simmons.html) that put 16 shows from The Ringer and Spotify Studios on Netflix starting in early 2026. The lineup leaned on sports, culture, and true crime: *The Bill Simmons Podcast*, *The Rewatchables*, *The Zach Lowe Show*, *Conspiracy Theories*, and more. By January 2026, Netflix had [34 podcasts](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/netflix-podcasts-january-2026/) on the service, including shows from Barstool and iHeartMedia.

Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos summed it up in one line: "Podcasting is becoming much more video-forward."

He is late to say it. YouTube [passed one billion monthly podcast viewers](https://androidcentral.com/apps-software/youtube-is-now-the-most-frequently-used-podcast-client-in-the-us) in February 2025 and is now the most-used podcast platform in the US. The podcast became a TV show a while ago. Netflix just made it official.

For a brand, the takeaway is simple. The biggest entertainment platforms in the world are paying for hours of people talking, because that is what their audiences watch. Your audience is the same audience.

## The Mistake: Treating Short-Form as the Whole Strategy

Somewhere in the last few years, a lot of founders and CMOs decided nobody watches anything longer than 30 seconds anymore. I suspect some of that came from executives with a 12-year-old barricaded in their room scrolling TikTok.

Imagine a studio head saying "Nobody watches shows anymore. Only trailers." They would be escorted out by security.

Swap "shows" for "long-form content" and "trailers" for "Reels," and the same argument gets nods in a lot of marketing meetings.

Short-form drives most of the views on platforms like YouTube. Long-form drives a huge share of the time people actually spend there. Those are two different jobs, and the brands that win run both.

## Short-Form Is the Text. Long-Form Is the Lunch.

The easiest way I have found to explain this to founders is friendship.

Think about the friend who always makes you laugh or think. You grab lunch every few weeks. In between, you trade texts. Quick hits. A meme, a "did you see this," a voice note.

The texts keep you top of mind. The lunches are where the friendship actually lives.

Texts without the lunches don't mean much. There is no emotional context to ground them. Get a steady stream of both, and you have a friend for life.

Content works the same way:

- **Short-form content** is the text. It earns attention, keeps your brand in the feed, and reminds people you exist.
- **Long-form content**, like a video podcast, is the lunch. It is where someone goes from recognizing your name to trusting your judgment.

I spent years building audience at Pandora, Twitch, and Amazon. The relationships that lasted, with artists, creators, and fans, were built in long-form moments: the stream, the live show, the long conversation. The clips spread them. The long-form is what people fell for.

## Why Video Podcasts Build Fandom (and Not Just Followers)

A follower recognizes you. A fan defends you, buys from you, and recruits their friends.

Fandom needs time. Specifically, it needs time spent with a person and a point of view. A good video podcast delivers exactly that:

1. **Parasocial trust.** An hour with the same voices every week creates familiarity that no 15-second ad can.
2. **A point of view.** Long conversations reveal how a team thinks, which is the one thing competitors cannot copy.
3. **Lore.** Running jokes, recurring segments, and inside references give an audience something to belong to.
4. **Ritual.** A weekly episode becomes a habit. Habits are the backbone of fandom.

This is why sports media, music, and creator brands lean so hard into the format. The Ringer's audience shows up for the people, then sticks around for the brand.

## The Rotisserie Chicken Content Strategy

The objection I hear most from founders: "We don't have time for long-form. We just need some quick Reels out there."

I don't buy it. A long-form episode is the most efficient thing your content team can make, if you treat it right.

Your content strategy should look like a rotisserie chicken. You roast it once and you get tonight's dinner, tomorrow's sandwiches, and stock for the rest of the week.

Uploading the full episode to YouTube is one drumstick. Here is the rest of the bird:

| From one episode | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Full transcript | A blog post built for SEO and AI search (GEO) |
| Three to eight strong moments | Vertical clips for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts |
| Hot takes and quotable lines | Quote graphics and text posts for LinkedIn and X |
| Key insights | The core of your next newsletter |
| Audio pulls | Audio ads and pre-roll |
| Product moments | Cutdowns for paid social campaigns |

One roast. A week of content.

Feed your audience nothing but dino nuggets and they eventually go looking for a real meal. They will find one. It just won't be in your kitchen.

## How to Launch a Brand Video Podcast That Works

If you are a founder or marketing leader at a media, entertainment, sports, or creator-economy company, here is how I would approach it.

### 1. Earn the hour before you ask for it

Start with a point of view. What do you believe about your category that most people get wrong? If you can't fill 10 episodes with that, you aren't ready. Interview-only shows with no perspective tend to sound like everyone else.

### 2. Pick the host your audience already trusts

That may be the founder, a creator you partner with, or someone on your team who is already the voice of your community. Audiences follow people. Pick the person who is the best company, and title can be secondary.

### 3. Build the system before the first episode

Decide in advance how every episode becomes clips, posts, newsletter content, and ads, and who owns each piece. The repurposing workflow is where the return on the format comes from. This is the core of the [Compound Growth System](/guides/compound-growth-system/): one piece of anchor content feeding every channel.

### 4. Commit to six months

Podcasts compound. The first 10 episodes mostly teach you what your show is. Budget and plan for consistency well past the point where it feels slow.

### 5. Measure depth, then reach

Track average watch time, returning viewers, and how often clips drive people back to full episodes. Then watch the downstream signals: newsletter sign-ups, branded search, and inbound conversations. Downloads alone will tell you very little.

## What This Means for Founders

When founders tell me "content is king" and "we need a TikTok strategy," both statements are true. What happens next is where it goes wrong.

A reactive marketer sprints to TikTok with a fistful of Canva templates, chasing views nobody remembers. A good marketer zooms out, builds the system, and reminds you that sometimes you need more than 30 seconds to make an impact.

Netflix and Spotify just bet real money that people will sit and watch other people talk for an hour. Your audience will do the same for you, if you give them something worth the time.

30 seconds can get attention. It takes longer than that to leave a mark.

## Takeaways

- **Video is where podcasts live now.** YouTube is the top US podcast platform, and Netflix streams podcasts as shows.
- **Short-form and long-form do different jobs.** Short-form earns attention. Long-form earns trust. Build both as one system.
- **Long-form is your most efficient content.** One episode can fuel a week of blog, social, newsletter, and paid content.
- **Start with a point of view and a trusted host.** Without them, you have a podcast that sounds like everyone else's.
- **Measure depth before reach.** Watch time and returning viewers predict fandom better than downloads.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should my brand start a video podcast?

Start one if you have a point of view worth an hour of someone's time and the discipline to publish on a schedule for at least six months. A video podcast works best as the anchor of a content system, where each episode is cut into short clips, blog posts, newsletter content, and ads. If you only want short-form reach, a podcast is the wrong tool.

### Why are video podcasts growing?

Distribution moved to video. YouTube passed one billion monthly podcast viewers in early 2025 and is the most-used podcast platform in the US. Netflix began streaming video podcasts from Spotify, The Ringer, and others in 2026. Audiences now discover shows through clips and then watch full episodes on TVs and phones.

### What is the difference between short-form and long-form content for brands?

Short-form content earns attention and keeps a brand top of mind. Long-form content builds trust and relationships, because the audience spends real time with a person and a point of view. According to David Hampian, the two work best as one system: long-form creates the material and the emotional context, and short-form distributes it.

### How do you repurpose a video podcast episode?

Treat each episode as raw material. The transcript becomes a blog post for SEO and AI search. Strong moments become vertical clips. Quotable lines become social graphics. Key insights become newsletter content. Audio cuts can become ads. One recording can fuel a week or more of content across channels.

### How do you measure a brand podcast?

Measure depth before reach: average watch time per episode, returning viewers, subscriber growth, and how often clips drive people back to full episodes. Then track downstream signals like newsletter sign-ups, inbound leads, and branded search. Download counts alone miss what a podcast is actually for.
