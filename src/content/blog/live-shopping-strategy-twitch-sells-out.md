Four years before TikTok Shop, there was Twitch Sells Out.

It's 2019. I'm staring at a spreadsheet I am sure is going to ruin me. Twenty-nine creators. Their verticals. More than 100 products. The deals we had closed, and the list of Amazon category leads who now had to configure every single one of them.

I remember the exact thought: "I'm not sleeping tonight, am I?"

We were about to run a 36-hour live shopping marathon on Twitch for Amazon Prime Day. Think QVC, with Twitch's irreverence. Creators selling live, on a platform whose audience is famous for roasting anything that smells like a sales pitch.

There was no playbook. "Live commerce" wasn't even a phrase people used yet.

We went in braced to get roasted out of the room. And then they bought. A lot.

## Live Shopping in 2026: The Numbers

Live shopping is the practice of selling products in real time during a livestream, where viewers can ask questions, react, and buy without leaving the stream. You will also hear it called live commerce or livestream shopping.

In the US, it has finally hit escape velocity:

- EMARKETER estimates US livestream ecommerce sales hit about **$14.6 billion in 2025**, up nearly 50% from the year before ([EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/insights/livestreaming-trends-stats)).
- **Whatnot** expected to pass **$6 billion in sales** in 2025, and its Black Friday sales roughly tripled year over year.
- About **one in five US digital buyers** purchased through a livestream in 2025.
- Even so, live shopping is still only about **5% of US ecommerce**, compared to roughly 60% in China.

Translation: the category is real, it is growing fast, and most brands haven't figured it out yet. That is a window. Windows close.

## What Happened When We Sold Live on Twitch

Here's what Twitch Sells Out looked like:

- **36 hours** of continuous live programming, built as 18 two-hour segments
- **29 creators** paired with products they actually used
- **100+ products**, purchasable in-stream through Amazon without leaving Twitch
- **7 of the top 10 products** sourced directly from manufacturers as exclusive Twitch deals

And the results:

- **$24 million** in incremental Prime Day sales
- **About 89,000** new Prime sign-ups, a 19% lift, with roughly $10 million in subscription value on top
- Close to **$1 million in combined value for every hour** we were live

You can see the full breakdown in the [Twitch Sells Out case study](/case-studies/twitch-sells-out/).

The most useful thing we walked away with was a lesson about why people bought.

## The Two Conditions That Make Live Commerce Work

People who hate being sold to still love to buy stuff. They just need two things to be true.

### 1. They have to like and trust the person selling

Twitch viewers didn't trust Amazon. They trusted their streamer. When a creator they had watched for hundreds of hours picked up a product they genuinely used and talked about it like a friend would, it landed as a recommendation. No ad was going to do that.

Trust is the asset you are borrowing in live shopping. You can't fake it, and you can't rent it for one afternoon. It comes from the host's existing relationship with the audience.

### 2. The experience has to be fun

We built Twitch Sells Out as entertainment first. Satire, chaos, challenges, chat interaction, and products woven into a show people wanted to watch anyway. Viewers stayed for the show and bought along the way.

The moment a livestream feels like a sales pitch, chat turns on you. When it feels like the best thing on, people hang around, and people who hang around buy.

### When both are true, price stops running the decision

This was the real lesson. When people trust the host and enjoy the experience, they stop comparison shopping. The buying decision becomes emotional and social: *my person likes this, and I want to be part of this moment.* Price drops down the list.

Whatnot is a whole business built on this. TikTok Shop is built on this. The live commerce wave everyone is chasing right now sits on a truth we watched play out live in 2019.

## How to Build a Live Shopping Strategy for Your Brand

If you are a founder with something to sell, here is how I would approach it.

### Step 1: Find the trusted host before the platform

Most brands start by asking, "Should we be on TikTok Shop?" Start instead with: "Who does our audience already trust?" That could be a creator in your category, your founder, a power user, or a community member people already follow. No trusted host, no live shopping strategy.

### Step 2: Design a show people would watch without buying

Write the format before you write the product list. What's the hook? What's the recurring segment? What happens in chat? If the stream wouldn't be worth watching with checkout turned off, it isn't ready.

### Step 3: Pick the platform where your buyers already gather

- **Whatnot** for collectibles, trading cards, sneakers, and enthusiast categories with a "hunt" built in
- **TikTok Shop** for broad consumer reach that pairs live selling with short video
- **YouTube or Twitch** when you already have a loyal community watching there
- **Amazon Live** when your catalog already lives on Amazon

Pick one. Win there before you expand.

### Step 4: Make the offer feel like an event

Exclusives, limited drops, live-only bundles, and products you can't get anywhere else. On Twitch Sells Out, sourcing exclusive manufacturer deals turned the event from a cost center into a revenue platform. Scarcity and timing give viewers a reason to buy now.

### Step 5: Run it as a series

One livestream is a stunt. A recurring show is a channel. The audience learns when to show up, the host gets better, and you learn which products and segments actually convert.

### Step 6: Measure past the first sale

Track conversion per hour live, average order value, and repeat buyers across shows. Then look at what the stream does for everything else: new subscribers, email sign-ups, and halo sales outside the featured products. On Prime Day, the halo across the rest of Amazon was a big part of the story.

## Common Live Shopping Mistakes

- **Treating it as a channel test with a junior host.** The host is the strategy.
- **Running an infomercial.** If it sounds like a pitch, the audience leaves.
- **Chasing the platform before the audience.** TikTok Shop is a tool, and trust in the host is what makes it work.
- **Doing it once.** Live commerce compounds through repetition.
- **Ignoring the community after the show.** The people who showed up are your most valuable audience. Follow up with them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is live shopping?

Live shopping, also called live commerce or livestream shopping, is selling products in real time during a livestream, with viewers able to ask questions and buy without leaving the stream. Platforms include TikTok Shop, Whatnot, YouTube, Amazon Live, and Twitch. It combines entertainment, a trusted host, and instant checkout.

### Does live shopping work for brands in the US?

Yes, and it is growing fast. EMARKETER estimates US livestream ecommerce sales reached about $14.6 billion in 2025, up nearly 50% year over year, and Whatnot expected more than $6 billion in sales in 2025. Live shopping is still roughly 5% of US ecommerce, which means most categories are early.

### What makes a live shopping event successful?

Two conditions. The audience has to like and trust the person selling, and the experience has to be fun to watch. According to David Hampian, who led the go-to-market for Twitch Sells Out, when both are true, price stops being the main factor in the buying decision.

### What was Twitch Sells Out?

Twitch Sells Out was a 36-hour live shopping marathon on Twitch for Amazon Prime Day 2019. Twenty-nine creators sold more than 100 products live, with in-stream purchasing through Amazon. It drove $24 million in incremental Prime Day sales and about 89,000 new Prime sign-ups, four years before TikTok Shop launched in the US.

### Should my brand use TikTok Shop, Whatnot, or its own livestream?

Go where your trusted hosts and your buyers already are. Whatnot is strongest for collectibles, trading cards, and enthusiast categories. TikTok Shop reaches a broad audience and pairs live selling with short video. Your own stream on YouTube or Twitch makes sense when you already have a loyal community there. Start with one platform and one recurring show.
