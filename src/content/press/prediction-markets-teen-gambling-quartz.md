## Context


Quartz consulted David Hampian as a gaming and digital product expert for a feature examining how prediction market platforms like Kalshi are drawing in younger users — including 18-to-20-year-olds largely blocked from traditional sports betting. Hampian's commentary draws on his time as VP of marketing at Hard Rock Bet and Twitch, giving him a direct view of how engagement-driven product design pulls in young male audiences and where the line between entertainment and gambling breaks down.


## Why This Matters


Prediction markets occupy a regulatory gray zone: they argue federal commodity oversight preempts state gambling law, leaving age restrictions looser than at licensed sportsbooks. Hampian's analysis reframes the risk as a product-design problem — the same mechanics that made digital platforms "sticky" for young users are now being applied to wagering, repackaged so it "feels like informed opinion rather than gambling." For an audience weighing the social cost of these platforms, that framing identifies why younger users are especially exposed.


## David Hampian's Expertise


Hampian brings firsthand experience from inside both gaming and engagement-driven digital platforms. At Hard Rock Bet he saw how thin the line between "entertainment" and "gambling" becomes when platforms are designed around engagement loops, social validation, and low-friction onboarding. His work at Twitch gave him direct insight into the UX mechanics that make platforms compelling for 18-to-24-year-old males — the same playbook he argues prediction markets are now borrowing from.
