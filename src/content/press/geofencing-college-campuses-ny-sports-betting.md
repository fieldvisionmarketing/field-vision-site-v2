## Context


Ball Is Life consulted David Hampian as a sports betting and digital operations expert for a feature analyzing proposed New York legislation (A10526/S10470) that would geofence college and university campuses out of the state's online sports betting system. Hampian's commentary draws on his direct experience launching Hard Rock Bet's online sportsbook in Florida, giving him a practitioner's view of what campus-level geolocation enforcement would actually require — and where it would break down.


## Why This Matters


Geolocation compliance is already a core part of how legal sportsbooks operate, enforcing state-line boundaries to satisfy regulators. The New York proposal would push that precision down to the campus level — a dramatically tighter standard. Hampian frames the question not as whether it is technically possible, but whether the operational cost, customer-experience friction, and edge-case failure rate make it workable in practice. His analysis reframes a policy debate as a digital operations problem.


## David Hampian's Expertise


Hampian brings hands-on experience in sports betting launch strategy and the digital operations behind regulated gaming. His work launching Hard Rock Bet's online sportsbook in Florida gave him direct exposure to how gaming companies manage state-line geofencing for regulatory compliance — and to the customer-experience tradeoffs that come with tightening location accuracy. That practitioner background lets him speak to both the technical feasibility and the real-world consequences of campus-level restrictions.


## Key Themes


- **Technically feasible, operationally messy**: Why campus-level geofencing can be built but is far harder to run than state-line enforcement
- **Edge cases multiply**: How tightening geolocation precision dramatically increases false positives — a bettor in an adjacent apartment building or passing through on public transit getting blocked
- **Customer-experience cost**: Why false positives create a support and retention problem that operators, not regulators, end up fielding
- **Compliance vs. usability**: The tradeoff between satisfying a stricter regulatory standard and keeping the legal product usable for legitimate bettors
