## Context


The CMO Wire featured David Hampian in a piece on why so many brands fall behind the culture they are trying to reach. Culture now moves in days, while most marketing teams still plan in quarters. By the time a trend shows up in a dashboard, clears an approval chain, and turns into a campaign, the audience has moved on. Hampian argues that no amount of sharp creative fixes a timing problem that lives in the org chart.


## Why This Matters


Most brands call themselves audience first. Hampian gives marketing leaders a way to check that claim against how the team actually operates, long before any campaign launches. He points to three places to look: the calendar, the budget, and the approval chain. If all three are locked, the brand can only react to moments after they are over.


> "The best moment your brand will have next year hasn't happened yet. Leave money for it, and give the people closest to your fans the authority to spend it. You won't regret it."


## David Hampian's Expertise


Hampian built his view of audience-first marketing at Twitch, where culture surfaced from chat, moderators, and streamers rather than from a marketing conference room. That approach led to the creator co-streams of Thursday Night Football, which grew viewership 73% year over year and previewed the format later popularized by ESPN's Manningcast. ## Key Themes


- **The calendar test**: Set dates still matter. A brand that runs only on a locked calendar has no way to join the conversation happening this week.
- **The budget test**: Audience-first brands hold reserves with no campaign attached, so they can show up when an unexpected moment breaks.
- **The approval test**: If only the CMO or an executive can say yes, the company is hierarchical. Authority should sit with the people closest to the community, including endemic creators brought on as consultants.
- **Listening beats dashboards**: Massive engagement numbers on a viral post usually mean the moment has already passed. Catching moments early takes real-time social listening and community management from people who live in those spaces.
- **Trend decks make followers**: A team whose only window into culture is a weekly trends deck will run six to eight weeks behind the people inside the community.
- **Treat playbooks as hypotheses**: Lifecycle programs that worked before still need testing in a new community. Brands that stay curious and experiment learn faster than brands that assume they already have the answer.
- **Data infrastructure**: Many teams sit on strong customer data they cannot use because consent mechanisms and backend schemas were never built to connect it to their marketing tools.
